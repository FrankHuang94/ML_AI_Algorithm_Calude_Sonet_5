# Long-Term Outlook (2-5 Years)

This file covers more speculative, longer-horizon directions than [near-term-outlook.md](near-term-outlook.md) — roughly 2-5 years out from **November 2026**, i.e., about 2029-2031. The further out a prediction reaches, the less confidence this repository has in it. Every forward-looking claim here is explicitly `[Projection]`, and this file leans more heavily on "here's a plausible direction and why" than "here's what will happen." Where a direction that earlier editions treated as speculative has since become established fact, it is moved out of projection and stated as grounded, with its evidence.

## How much attention does a model need?

**What changed since the mid-2026 edition.** The mid-2026 edition framed this section as "alternative architectures to attention at scale" and treated hybrid designs as a plausible 2-5 year development. That was too cautious: hybrids were already in production. The question has accordingly shifted from *"will something replace attention?"* to *"what fraction of a model's layers need full attention, and in what form?"*

**Grounded — where things stand (November 2026).** Linear-attention and state-space layers (see [state-space-models.md](../03-deep-learning-architectures/state-space-models.md)) keep a fixed-size recurrent state instead of a KV cache that grows with every token, which makes long contexts cheap but means the layer can only keep a compressed summary of the past. Full softmax attention can look up any earlier token exactly, at a cost that grows with context length. Production models now mix the two:

- **Hybrid linear + full attention at roughly 3:1.** Qwen3-Next (2025) interleaves Gated DeltaNet layers (a linear-attention variant with a decaying, "delta-rule" corrected state) with gated full-attention layers at about 3:1; the Qwen3.5 line (early 2026) uses the same schedule, e.g., a 60-layer model built as 15 repeats of [3 Gated DeltaNet + 1 attention] (reported). Kimi Linear (2025) pairs Kimi Delta Attention with MLA layers at about 3:1. NVIDIA's Nemotron-H / Nemotron Nano 2 (2025) mix Mamba-2 and attention layers, following AI21's earlier Jamba (2024).
- **Local/global interleaving.** Sliding-window (local) attention layers alternate with global attention layers (e.g., Gemma 2/3, gpt-oss).
- **Compressed and sparse full attention.** Even the "full" attention can be made cheaper: DeepSeek Sparse Attention (2025) attends only to top-k tokens chosen by a lightweight indexer, and DeepSeek-V4 (April 2026, reported) interleaves compressed-sparse and heavily compressed attention with a sliding window of recent tokens.
- **Making attention itself better-behaved.** Gated attention (Qwen, 2025; NeurIPS 2025 best paper) adds an elementwise sigmoid gate on the attention output, which improves training stability and removes "attention sinks" (tokens, often the first one, that soak up a large share of attention weight without carrying useful content).

**`[Projection]`** Over 2-5 years, this repository expects the attention ratio and placement — how many fixed-state layers per full-attention layer, where the full-attention layers sit, and whether they are dense, sparse or compressed — to become an ordinary architecture hyperparameter tuned per model, much like depth and width are today, rather than a binary architectural choice. `[Projection]` A frontier general-purpose model with *no* full-attention-style layers at all remains, in this repository's view, less likely within the window than continued hybrids, because exact retrieval over long contexts is precisely what fixed-size states are worst at. `[Projection]` The share of compute and memory spent on exact, dense, all-pairs attention is expected to keep shrinking, through some combination of higher linear-to-full ratios and sparse/compressed attention inside the remaining full layers. This repository holds these projections with real uncertainty: most closed frontier models do not disclose their architectures, so public evidence on how hybrids compare to pure-attention models at the very largest scale is limited, and the right ratio may well differ by task (retrieval-heavy versus generation-heavy workloads).

## Continual / online learning approaches

**The current limitation.** Frontier models are still, overwhelmingly, trained in large discrete pretraining/post-training cycles and then deployed with fixed weights — they don't continuously update their own weights from ongoing deployment the way a human keeps learning from experience. This is directly tied to the catastrophic forgetting problem in [open-problems.md](open-problems.md): naively continuing to train a deployed model on new data risks degrading what it already knows.

**Grounded — early research since mid-2025.** Two research directions are worth naming, both still early:

- **Nested Learning and the Hope architecture** (Google, NeurIPS 2025). This framing treats a model as a set of nested optimization problems whose parameters update at different frequencies — some fast (adapting within a context), some slow (changing only in training) — giving a spectrum of memory timescales the authors call "continuum memory." Hope, a variant of the earlier Titans architecture, reported continual-learning results at small scale (roughly 760M-1.3B parameters).
- **Test-time training** — updating some of a model's weights *during inference*, using the input itself as training signal, so the model adapts to the document or task in front of it. In-Place Test-Time Training (ByteDance Seed / Peking University, ICLR 2026 oral) updates a designated subset of "fast weights" at inference time.

Neither has been shown to solve continual learning at frontier scale; as of November 2026 the problem remains unsolved there.

**`[Projection]`** Meaningful progress on practical continual learning remains plausible over 2-5 years, motivated by the obvious value of models that can absorb new information without a full retraining cycle — but this repository still treats confident claims of an imminent general solution with real skepticism. `[Projection]` The most likely path is incremental and layered: multi-timescale memory and test-time-training ideas of the kind above scaled up and combined with parameter-efficient updates (see [finetuning-and-peft.md](../05-training-methodology/finetuning-and-peft.md)) and with retrieval, which avoids updating weights at all for many kinds of new information. `[Projection]` A useful early marker would be a frontier-scale model that improves on a task during deployment *and* demonstrably does not lose capability elsewhere — which is exactly the property small-scale results cannot yet establish.

## Test-time compute scaling

**Grounded — now established fact.** The mid-2026 edition listed this as "emerging, actively researched"; it is better described as an established scaling axis. OpenAI's o1 (September 2024) was the first widely deployed "reasoning model" — a model trained (largely with RL) to generate a long chain of intermediate reasoning before answering — and showed that accuracy keeps improving as the model is allowed more "thinking" tokens at inference time. DeepSeek-R1 (January 2025) reproduced the recipe openly with RL on verifiable rewards, and R1-Zero showed long chains of thought emerging from RL on a base model without supervised fine-tuning (see [rl-for-llms.md](../07-reinforcement-learning/rl-for-llms.md)). In July 2025, experimental reasoning models from Google DeepMind and OpenAI reported gold-medal-level performance at the International Mathematical Olympiad. The mid-2026 projection that systems would learn to decide *how much* computation a problem warrants has also partly materialized at the system level: GPT-5 (August 2025) routes between a fast mode and a reasoning mode. This axis complements, rather than replaces, the pretraining scaling laws covered in [transformer-architecture.md](../03-deep-learning-architectures/transformer-architecture.md).

**What remains open.** That more inference compute helps is settled. Still open: the precise quantitative form and limits of the trade-off (how far gains continue before flattening, and how this varies by domain), how to split a fixed budget between a larger pretrained model and more thinking, and how well test-time compute transfers to domains without automatic checkers.

**`[Projection]`** Over 2-5 years, this repository expects adaptive allocation to move from coarse routing between modes toward finer-grained control inside a single model — spending more deliberation on hard steps and less on easy ones within one task. `[Projection]` The center of gravity for test-time compute is expected to shift from single hard questions (competition math) toward long agentic tasks, where "thinking" includes acting, checking results and retrying, so test-time scaling and agentic reliability (see [open-problems.md](open-problems.md)) become increasingly the same problem. `[Projection]` Whether the field arrives at test-time scaling laws as predictive as pretraining scaling laws is still, in this repository's view, a genuinely open empirical question.

## A methodological note on this file's uncertainty

Per this repository's ground rules (see [scope-and-methodology.md](../00-overview/scope-and-methodology.md)), the further a claim reaches into the future, the less this repository asks you to trust it as anything beyond "a reasonable person's extrapolation from current public trends." This edition's own corrections illustrate the point: the mid-2026 edition placed hybrid architectures 2-5 years out when they were already shipping, and treated test-time compute scaling as more tentative than the evidence by then warranted. This file's job is to give a plausible, informed shape to the next few years, not to pretend to a certainty this repository — or, frankly, anyone publicly writing about the field in late 2026 — actually has.

| Direction | Current status (November 2026) | `[Projection]` for 2-5 years out (~2029-2031) |
|---|---|---|
| How much attention is needed (hybrids, sparse/compressed attention) | Hybrids in production at ~3:1 linear-to-full ratios; sparse/compressed attention shipping in open models | Attention ratio/placement becomes a routine tuned hyperparameter; dense all-pairs attention's share keeps shrinking; fully attention-free frontier models still considered less likely |
| Continual/online learning | Early research (Nested Learning/Hope, test-time training); unsolved at frontier scale | Incremental, layered progress plausible; full solution not confidently expected |
| Test-time compute scaling | Established scaling axis (o1, R1, IMO 2025 gold-level results); coarse adaptive routing deployed | Finer-grained adaptive allocation; merges with long-horizon agentic work; predictive "laws" still uncertain |
