# Near-Term Outlook (6-18 Months)

This file covers trends this repository expects to continue or accelerate over roughly the next six to eighteen months, **as of November 2026** — that is, a window running roughly through mid-2028. It replaces the mid-2026 edition of this file, whose projections are checked against what actually happened in the scorecard below. Per this repository's methodology (see [scope-and-methodology.md](../00-overview/scope-and-methodology.md)), every forward-looking claim here is labeled `[Projection]` — treat these as reasoned extrapolations from public research trends and widely-reported industry direction, not settled fact. Where a claim is grounded in already-published research or publicly reported releases rather than extrapolation, that's noted explicitly as such, and anything known only from a vendor's own report is marked "reported."

## Scorecard: how the mid-2026 projections held up

**Grounded — a look back, not a projection.** The mid-2026 edition made four near-term projections. Two caveats before grading them: only about five months have passed, which is short relative to a 6-18 month window, and several of the developments below were already under way before mid-2026. So this scorecard checks *direction* (did the trend continue the way the file said it would?), not precision.

| Mid-2026 projection | Verdict (November 2026) | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Inference cost keeps falling incrementally, via several compounding techniques | **Held up**, but the mix of drivers differed from what was emphasized | 4-bit floating-point formats, sparse/compressed attention, MTP/EAGLE-style speculation, disaggregated serving (details below) |
| Context windows keep growing; *usability* of long context matters more than the headline number | **Held up** | 1M-token context reached open-weight models (DeepSeek-V4, reported); how well that context is actually used remains the real question |
| Rapid but uneven progress in agentic / tool-use training | **Held up** | Agentic RL in code sandboxes and tool environments became standard; "RL environments" became a major investment area; reliability is still uneven |
| MoE adoption keeps broadening at the largest scale; dense models persist for smaller use cases | **Held up, and went further than stated** | Ultra-sparse MoE models with roughly 1T or more total parameters but only a few percent active per token |

**Inference cost — what actually drove it.** The mid-2026 file emphasized sub-4-bit post-training quantization and draft-model-free speculative decoding. The bigger observed movers were somewhat different:

- **4-bit floating-point "microscaling" formats with hardware support.** A microscaling format stores numbers in small blocks that share one scale factor, so each individual value can use very few bits. MXFP4 uses 32-element blocks with a power-of-two scale; NVFP4 uses 16-element blocks with an FP8 scale plus one FP32 scale per tensor (see [quantization.md](../06-inference-optimization/quantization.md)). OpenAI's gpt-oss open-weight MoE models (August 2025) shipped with their expert weights natively in MXFP4, and NVIDIA reported pretraining a 12B hybrid Mamba-Transformer on 10T tokens in NVFP4 with MMLU-Pro of 62.58% versus 62.62% for an FP8 baseline (arXiv 2509.25149) — i.e., 4-bit moved from "compress after training" toward "train and serve natively."
- **Sparse and compressed attention.** Instead of every query attending to every past token, DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DeepSeek-V3.2, 2025) uses a small "lightning indexer" to score past tokens and lets each query attend only to the top-k of them. DeepSeek-V4 (technical report, April 2026, reported) goes further with Compressed Sparse Attention and Heavily Compressed Attention, which merge groups of tokens into compressed KV entries, and reports about 27% of the single-token FLOPs and about 10% of the KV cache of V3.2 at the same long context (see [kv-cache-and-attention-optimization.md](../06-inference-optimization/kv-cache-and-attention-optimization.md)).
- **Speculation built into the model.** Multi-Token Prediction (MTP — an extra training head that predicts more than one future token; DeepSeek-V3, 2024) can be reused as a speculative-decoding draft, and EAGLE-3 (2025) drafts from the target model's own internal features rather than from a separate small model (see [speculative-decoding.md](../06-inference-optimization/speculative-decoding.md)).
- **Disaggregated serving.** Running the compute-bound prefill phase (processing the prompt) and the memory-bandwidth-bound decode phase (generating tokens one at a time) on separate GPU pools went mainstream in 2025 (DistServe, Mooncake, NVIDIA Dynamo), alongside prefix caching that reuses the KV cache of shared prompt prefixes (see [serving-and-batching.md](../06-inference-optimization/serving-and-batching.md)).

The lesson for this edition: a meaningful share of the cost reduction came from *model architecture* (what the attention layers compute and how much state they keep), not just from serving-side tricks applied to a fixed architecture.

**What the mid-2026 edition underweighted.** Two developments were treated as more distant than they turned out to be. First, hybrid linear-attention architectures were already in production models (Qwen3-Next, 2025; Kimi Linear, 2025; NVIDIA Nemotron-H, 2025), yet [long-term-outlook.md](long-term-outlook.md) placed hybrids in its 2-5 year window. Second, the post-training pattern of training several specialist RL models and then distilling them into one student (on-policy distillation, below) was not mentioned at all. Both are corrected in this edition.

## Inference cost reduction

**Grounded in current trajectory.** As the scorecard shows, the techniques in [06-inference-optimization](../06-inference-optimization/) — quantization, KV-cache reduction (GQA, Multi-head Latent Attention), sparse/compressed attention, speculative decoding, continuous batching, disaggregated serving, prefix caching — are now deployed together, and increasingly designed into the model itself rather than bolted on afterwards.

**`[Projection]`** Continued, compounding (not step-change) reduction in cost-per-token at a given quality level through mid-2028, with the largest contributions coming from the specific trends broken out below (4-bit numerics, sparse/compressed attention, hybrid layouts) plus continued hardware-software co-design. `[Projection]` Total spending on inference is not expected to fall in step with cost-per-token, because reasoning models and agents consume many more tokens per task than single-shot chat — cheaper tokens are likely to be spent on more thinking and longer agent runs (see the compute-cost discussion in [open-problems.md](open-problems.md)).

## 4-bit training and serving moving to the mainstream

**Grounded in current trajectory.** FP8 mixed-precision training was demonstrated at frontier scale by DeepSeek-V3 (December 2024). 4-bit then reached shipped weights (gpt-oss in MXFP4, August 2025) and a 10T-token pretraining run (NVIDIA's NVFP4 study, 2025, revised March 2026). The NVFP4 recipe needed several stabilizing tricks — random Hadamard transforms (a cheap orthogonal rotation that spreads outlier values across a block so one large value doesn't wreck the shared scale), 2-D block scaling, stochastic rounding on gradients, keeping a few sensitive layers in higher precision, and switching to higher precision late in training — which is a reminder that 4-bit training is a recipe, not just a datatype.

**`[Projection]`** Through mid-2028, this repository expects 4-bit floating-point formats (NVFP4, MXFP4 or close successors) to become a common default for serving large open-weight models and to be used for a growing share of pretraining compute, rather than remaining a research demonstration. `[Projection]` Expect this to be a *mixed*-precision default — most matrix multiplications in 4-bit, with sensitive layers, optimizer state and late-training phases kept at higher precision — rather than "everything in 4 bits." `[Projection]` Sub-4-bit methods are expected to stay more niche, mainly for memory-constrained local and edge deployment, since hardware support is concentrated on 4-bit and 8-bit formats.

## Context length, hybrid layouts, and sparse/compressed attention

**Grounded in current trajectory.** Two architectural responses to the cost of long context reached production in 2025-26:

- **Hybrid linear-attention + full-attention layouts.** Linear attention replaces the ever-growing KV cache of softmax attention with a fixed-size state that is updated once per token, so per-token cost doesn't grow with context length (see [state-space-models.md](../03-deep-learning-architectures/state-space-models.md)); the price is that a fixed-size state can't remember everything exactly. Gated DeltaNet is a linear-attention variant whose state update both decays old content (the gate) and corrects stored associations rather than only adding to them (the "delta rule"). Hybrids interleave many such layers with a few full-attention layers that can still look up any past token exactly. Production examples: Qwen3-Next (2025, roughly 3 Gated DeltaNet layers per gated full-attention layer), the Qwen3.5 line (early 2026, same 3:1 schedule, reported), Kimi Linear (2025, ~3:1 with MLA layers), and NVIDIA's Nemotron-H / Nemotron Nano 2 (Mamba-2 + attention). Simpler cousins interleave sliding-window (local) attention with global attention layers (e.g., Gemma 2/3, gpt-oss).
- **Sparse and compressed attention** (DeepSeek-V3.2's DSA; DeepSeek-V4's compressed attention variants, reported), described in the scorecard above. DeepSeek-V4 also reports a 1M-token context window for open-weight models.

**`[Projection]`** Hybrid layouts — a majority of linear-attention (or other fixed-state) layers plus a minority of full-attention layers, on the order of the ~3:1 ratios seen so far — become a common default for new long-context *open-weight* models through mid-2028. (This repository makes no claim about undisclosed closed-model architectures.) `[Projection]` Sparse/compressed attention mechanisms of the DSA/CSA type spread beyond DeepSeek to other labs' long-context models, and the two ideas increasingly combine: the remaining "full" attention layers in a hybrid themselves become sparse or compressed. `[Projection]` Headline context length keeps creeping upward, but as in the mid-2026 edition, this repository considers *usability* — reliably retrieving and reasoning over information anywhere in a very long context, not just accepting it as input — the meaningful marker of progress. A 1M-token window does not by itself establish that the model uses all of it well, and this repository has no independent evidence that the usability gap has closed.

## Agentic and tool-use training, and on-policy distillation

**Grounded in current trajectory.** Since DeepSeek-R1 (January 2025) showed large-scale RL with verifiable rewards (rewards computed by a checker, e.g., running unit tests) working in the open, the field moved on to *agentic* RL: the model acts over many steps in code sandboxes and tool environments and is rewarded on the outcome (see [rl-for-llms.md](../07-reinforcement-learning/rl-for-llms.md)). Building such "RL environments" became a major investment area. A widely reported 2026 post-training pattern is multi-teacher on-policy distillation: train separate specialist RL models (math, code, agentic, and so on), then distill them into one student by sampling responses *from the student* and having the relevant teacher score each token, minimizing reverse KL divergence on the student's own trajectories. Sampling from the student matters because the student is corrected on the mistakes it actually makes, not just shown teacher-written text it would never produce itself; separating specialists avoids capabilities interfering with each other in one mixed RL run. (Underlying technique: on-policy distillation/GKD, Agarwal et al., 2023-24; survey: Lambert & Timbers, June 2026; examples reported include Nemotron 3 Ultra with more than ten teachers, and staged SFT → reasoning RL → agentic RL → general RL pipelines with cross-stage distillation in GLM-5.) See [pruning-and-distillation.md](../06-inference-optimization/pruning-and-distillation.md) for distillation basics.

**`[Projection]`** Through mid-2028, "specialist RL models, then on-policy distillation into a generalist" becomes a standard post-training recipe across labs that publish their methods, not a frontier-lab novelty. `[Projection]` Investment in RL environments continues, with the bottleneck shifting from RL algorithms (GRPO-family refinements such as DAPO and GSPO are already mature enough to use) toward environment quality: realistic tasks, reliable outcome checkers, and resistance to the model gaming the checker. `[Projection]` As in the mid-2026 edition, expect visibly improving capability paired with visible new failure modes — uneven, bumpy progress on long-horizon reliability rather than a smooth curve (see [open-problems.md](open-problems.md)).

## MoE and sparsity

**Grounded in current trajectory.** MoE (see [mixture-of-experts.md](../03-deep-learning-architectures/mixture-of-experts.md)) moved toward *ultra-sparse* configurations — very large total parameter counts with a small active fraction per token: Kimi K2 (2025, ~1T total / 32B active), DeepSeek-V4-Pro (2026, 1.6T total / 49B active, reported), and Qwen3-Next's ultra-sparse MoE. Supporting techniques matured alongside: auxiliary-loss-free load balancing (a per-expert bias on routing scores, nudged up or down to even out load, instead of an extra loss term; DeepSeek-V3), and the Muon optimizer at trillion-parameter scale with QK-clip to keep attention logits from exploding (MuonClip, Kimi K2).

**`[Projection]`** Ultra-sparse MoE remains the dominant design for the largest open-weight models through mid-2028, with active-parameter counts growing more slowly than total-parameter counts. `[Projection]` Dense models are still not expected to disappear: they remain the practical choice for small, latency-sensitive or on-device deployments, and as distillation targets, where MoE's memory footprint (all experts must be stored even though few are used per token) isn't justified.

## Diffusion language models in latency-sensitive niches

**Grounded in current trajectory.** Diffusion language models (dLLMs) generate text by starting from a fully masked (or noised) sequence and filling in many positions in parallel over a number of refinement steps, instead of strictly one token at a time left to right (see [diffusion-models.md](../04-generative-models/diffusion-models.md) and [autoregressive-generation.md](../04-generative-models/autoregressive-generation.md)). Examples: LLaDA (open 8B masked-diffusion LM, 2025), Inception Labs' Mercury (2025) and Mercury 2 (February 2026, marketed as a reasoning-capable dLLM at around 1,000 tokens/s — a vendor claim), and Google's Gemini Diffusion (experimental since May 2025, still waitlisted as of mid-2026, reported). An ICML 2026 Outstanding Paper ("The Flexibility Trap") argued that the arbitrary generation order dLLMs allow is less valuable than often assumed.

**`[Projection]`** Through mid-2028, dLLMs find real niches where latency and throughput matter more than peak quality — e.g., code completion and editing, inline tools, and fast drafting — but do not displace autoregressive models at the capability frontier. `[Projection]` A plausible convergence point is hybrid use: diffusion-style parallel drafting feeding or alongside autoregressive verification, competing with speculative decoding for the same "faster tokens" role.

## World models for agent and robot training

**Grounded in current trajectory.** A world model is a learned simulator: given the current state and an action, it predicts what happens next, so an agent can practice inside it (see [model-based-rl.md](../07-reinforcement-learning/model-based-rl.md)). Dreamer 4 (Hafner et al., 2025) learned to collect diamonds in Minecraft purely from offline data, training inside a learned world model. Google DeepMind's Genie 3 (August 2025) generates navigable, interactive worlds in real time from a text prompt with minutes of consistency; Genie 4 (research preview, 27 October 2026, reported) adds native audio, about two minutes of consistency (reported), and is being explored for rehearsing robot tasks in simulation.

**`[Projection]`** Through mid-2028, Genie-style interactive world models are increasingly used as *training environments* — generating varied practice scenarios for embodied agents and robots, and possibly for computer-use agents — as a supplement to hand-built simulators and real-world data. `[Projection]` This repository expects the main limitation to remain fidelity: policies trained in a learned simulator inherit its errors (physics that is plausible-looking but wrong, drift over long rollouts), so real-world validation stays necessary and world-model training is a complement, not a replacement.

## Gated and staged releases of cyber-capable models

**Grounded (reported).** In 2026, frontier labs began gating or staging the release of their most capable models because of offensive-cyber capability — for example, limiting initial access to vetted cyber defenders; this was reported for both Google and Anthropic models. A US Executive Order on frontier AI and cybersecurity followed on 2 June 2026 (reported).

**`[Projection]`** Through mid-2028, staged release — restricted early access for vetted users, followed by broader availability once safeguards and defensive use have had a head start — becomes a normal pattern for frontier models that cross capability thresholds in areas like cyber, rather than a one-off. `[Projection]` Expect this to put more weight on *pre-release* capability evaluation (deciding which tier a model belongs in before anyone outside the lab uses it), which inherits all the evaluation-validity problems discussed in [open-problems.md](open-problems.md), and to create a growing gap between what the most capable gated models can do and what is publicly accessible at any given moment.
